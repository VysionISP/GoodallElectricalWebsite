Safety

Signs your switchboard needs an upgrade.

12 August 2026 · 5 min read · Goodall Electrical Team

Plenty of homes across Gippsland — especially anything built before the mid-1990s — are still running on an original switchboard. It might have never given you a problem. That's not the same as it being safe.

The ceramic fuse box

If your switchboard still has round ceramic fuses with a rewireable fuse wire inside, it predates the modern safety switch entirely. These boards have no way of detecting a current leaking to earth — the exact fault that causes electric shock. A tripped circuit breaker is an inconvenience. A missing safety switch is a hazard.

No RCD (safety switch) protection

Since 1991, safety switches (RCDs) have been required on new and altered circuits in Victoria, but older homes are often only partially covered — power circuits protected, lighting circuits not, or vice versa. A full board check tells you exactly what's covered and what isn't.

  • Buzzing, warm, or discoloured switches at the board
  • Lights flickering when other appliances switch on
  • Breakers that trip repeatedly without an obvious cause
  • A board with no visible RCD/safety switch test buttons
  • Insurance or building inspections flagging the switchboard

What an upgrade actually involves

In most homes, a switchboard upgrade is a same-day job: new circuit breakers, RCDs across all circuit groups, a compliant enclosure, and a Certificate of Electrical Safety on completion. We'll walk you through exactly what your existing board is missing before any work starts — no surprise add-ons once the cover is off.

Renovating or adding solar/EV charging?

If you're planning a renovation, a solar install, or an EV charger, your existing board's capacity needs checking first — this is often the point where an ageing board gets found out. Better to plan the upgrade in than have it stall a later project.
