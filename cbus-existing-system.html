CBUS · Existing Systems

Found CBUS wall plates
in your new home or business?

If you've just moved into a property with unusual multi-button switches and no idea how they work, you've probably got an existing CBUS lighting control system. Here's what to check before you touch anything, and how we can get it working properly again.

How to Tell You've Got CBUS

The tell-tale signs.

None of these alone is proof, but two or three together are a strong sign there's a CBUS network in the walls.

Multi-button wall plates

Switches with several unlabelled or numbered buttons instead of a normal single or double switch.

Dimming with no dimmer knob

The lights clearly dim, but there's no visible slider or rotary dimmer at the switch.

A patch panel somewhere out of sight

A small panel with rows of terminals in a cupboard, roof space, meter box or comms rack.

Switches that don't match the room

A wall plate with buttons that don't obviously correspond to the light fittings actually in the room.

Mentions from the agent or previous owner

Anyone mentioning the smart switches or the lighting system during the sale or handover.

What To Do Next

Four things, in order.

01

Don't remove or rewire anything yourself

CBUS control cable looks different from standard 240V wiring and isn't a normal light circuit — treating it that way risks damaging the network or an electric shock.

02

Look for documentation

Check for a manual, an as-built wiring diagram, or an installer's contact left in a drawer, the meter box, or with the property manager or agent.

03

Test what you've got

Try each button on each wall plate and note what works, what doesn't, and what you don't understand. It saves time on the first site visit.

04

Get it assessed before renovating

If you're about to knock down a wall or rewire a room, get a CBUS electrician in first so you don't cut a control cable you didn't know was there.

FAQ

Common issues after moving in.

The scenes don't do what I expect anymore.

Very common after a change of ownership or tenancy — the programming still reflects how the last occupant used the space. We can walk through what's there and reprogram scenes to suit how you actually want to use each room.

We can't find anyone who knows the system.

That's fine — we don't need the original installer. We can trace the network, work out what's connected to what, and document it properly so you're never in this position again.

We want to add more switches or automate more rooms.

As long as the existing network is in reasonable condition, extending it is usually straightforward — running new cable to additional points and adding them to the existing controller.

A renovation disturbed some wiring and now part of it doesn't work.

This happens when trades who don't know CBUS cut or cap what looks like a spare cable. We can trace the break and reconnect or reroute the affected section.

We just want it removed and replaced with normal switches.

That's a valid option too — we can decommission a CBUS network and reinstate standard switching if that's genuinely what suits the property better. We'll give you an honest opinion either way before you commit.
